# Privacy

Clipshot collects nothing.

## What leaves your device

Nothing. This app has no internet access.

- **Clipboard.** When you paste, the app you paste into gets that screenshot. Nothing else is shared.
- **Capture** (optional, not in the Google Play version). The accessibility service cannot read your screen. It receives no events and only presses the screenshot button.
- **Backups.** Clipshot's data is excluded from cloud backups and phone-to-phone transfers.
- **Links.** Links in About open in your browser or mail app, only when you tap them. Clipshot itself never connects to anything.

The same list is in the app, under About, "What leaves your device".

## What stays on the phone

- It has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything anywhere.
- It reads images only to find the screenshot that was just taken. It ignores everything else.
- It keeps a private copy of the last three screenshots, so the clipboard keeps working. They never leave the app's private storage, except through the clipboard when you paste.
- It stores four settings and counters on the phone (on/off, remove from gallery, the last copied screenshot, how many were copied).
- Capture uses an accessibility service, because Android offers no other way for an app to take a screenshot. The service receives no accessibility events and cannot read the content of any window: it does not know what is on your screen, what you type or which app you use. Its only actions are closing the Quick Settings panel and pressing the screenshot button. It is off until you turn it on.

## Erasing

- **Erase everything** (in About) deletes the settings, counters and copies, cancels pending gallery removals, takes Clipshot's clip off the clipboard, turns off "Remove from gallery" and turns off the Capture service. Clipshot is back to its first launch.
- Uninstalling the app deletes all of it.
